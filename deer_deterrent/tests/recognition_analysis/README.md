# Analysis of the recorded recognition trials

This folder holds the analysis of the [recorded recognition trials of November 4, 2026](../recognition/README.md). Its Python scripts read the trial program's model file, [deer.espdl](../../experiments/deer-classifier/model/deer.espdl), from its own bytes and run it on a computer in integer arithmetic, written from the kernels of ESP-DL 3.3.14, the version the trial program builds with. That reader is checked first against the board's own records of the trials. The experiments then run it on the 74 saved model inputs and on edited copies of them (the ceiling light painted out, the picture flipped), on the prints cropped from the saved JPEGs and shrunk and moved over plain fields, on plain shapes drawn on plain fields, on blocky color pictures searched for the highest response of the two coarser layers, on the 175 camera photos saved in [../captures](../captures/) and on a phone photograph of the D2 print. Every model response here is computed on a computer; none is a run on the board. The scripts also measure the recorded boxes' sizes and positions, replay the two-of-three rule over the recorded scores, recompute the October 29 board run from its saved results, and calculate recall and false-alarm rates from the float model's evaluation counts in the training project's record (`e_q_misc.py`, below).

## The check against the board's records

`compare_board.py` runs the model on each of the 74 saved model inputs (`../recognition/*/*.input.i8`: the 68 frames of the main session and one frame each of set-up sessions 04 to 08 and of the practice session), decodes its boxes as the board's post-processing does (a floor of 0.10, which, rounded to the model's score steps, keeps scores from 0.0953 on the finest layer and from 0.1192 on the other two; overlap 0.70; at most 10 boxes; the 320 × 240 picture letterboxed into the 224 × 224 input) and compares each frame's score and boxes with the frame's own record. All 74 scores agree within 1e-6 and all 69 recorded boxes agree to the pixel. The script itself prints 71 of 74 full box-list matches because it compares scores rounded to six decimals: in three frames (main-session frames 64 and 67, set-up session 08) the board's value, computed in single precision, is 0.4378235042 and rounds to 0.437824, while the computer's, in double precision, is 0.4378234991 and rounds to 0.437823. `compare_board_exact.py` compares with a tolerance of 1e-6 and finds 74 of 74 frames with identical box lists, all 69 boxes from the finest of the model's three detection layers; a second decode it carries, written in single precision against ESP-DL's post-processing source, gives the same pixels, its scores differing from the records by one single-precision step (1.5e-8) in 11 frames. On these inputs no convolution's sum, bias included, exceeds 97,149 in magnitude, under a fifth of the −524,288 to 524,287 a 20-bit accumulator holds (`compare_board_exact.txt`), so holding it to 20 bits (`compare_board.py ... sat`) changes nothing, and the result is the same. 34 of the 74 frames have no box, so the 69 boxes, each a score and four coordinates, are the real test. The board recorded nothing inside the model, so the agreement is at its outputs.

## Rerun

From this folder, with Python 3, numpy and Pillow (`python -m pip install numpy pillow`). The results here were made that way on a copy of the repository, with Python 3.14.7, numpy 2.5.3 and Pillow 12.3.0. Every script reads the model, the recorded sessions, the board run of October 29 in [test-results](../../experiments/deer-classifier/test-results/), the detector photos and `inputs/` by paths relative to this folder and writes into `results/`. Each run's printed output is kept as `results/<script>.txt`, except that `dump_model.py`'s is `results/model_graph.txt`, `compare_board.py`'s two runs are `results/compare_board_plain.txt` and `results/compare_board_sat.txt`, and the four scripts that print only picture sizes (`sheet.py`, `sheet2.py`, `rectify_d2.py`, `patches.py`) keep none. Run them in this order, since `e_h_clean.py` reads what `rectify_d2.py` writes, `e_n_sweep.py` and `prediction_sweep.py` read what `e_h_clean.py` writes, and `e_q_misc.py` reads what `compare_board.py` writes:

One model run takes about 0.04 s here, and the whole list above takes about 23 minutes: most scripts take seconds, `e_k_polarity.py` half a minute, `e_h_clean.py`, `e_o_occl.py`, `e_r_wake.py` and `prediction_sweep.py` one to four minutes each, and `e_n_sweep.py`, with 16,800 model runs, about 11 minutes. `e_m_weights.py` and `e_r_wake.py` draw their random numbers from a fixed seed.

In the outputs D1, D2 and D3 are the three deer prints and N1 the dog print; `f6` is frame 6 of the main session `p3-demo-20261104-02`, `s07` and `s08` set-up sessions 07 and 08, and the practice frame is that of `d1-practice-20261104-01`. Distances are from lens to print. Widths are in model pixels, those of the 224 × 224 input, unless camera pixels, those of the 320 × 240 picture, are named; one model pixel is 10/7 camera pixels. "Raw" and "logit" are the model's output before the sigmoid, and a score is its sigmoid. In `e_e_lamp.py` and `lamp_crop.png`, "lamp" is the round ceiling light. A box counts as on the ceiling light when its left edge is at 200 camera pixels or more and its top at 195 or more, the rule of every script here that sorts boxes; on the main session's 58 recorded boxes it sorts every box as looking at the frames does, 47 on the light and 11 off it (`e_l_tally.txt`).

## What it shows

- **The model answers small things.** Every box on the 74 frames is 37 to 83 camera pixels wide, 12 to 26% of the picture's width, and comes from the finest layer; the middle layer stays below its lowest reportable score (at most 0.047) and the coarsest is silent, even with a print filling the picture (`e_q_misc.txt`, `e_a_heads.txt`). That is no absolute limit: on one of the 175 detector photos the middle layer gives a box 144 camera pixels wide, 45% of the picture, at 0.1824, on a bright window and the wall around it (`e_j_captures.txt`, `e_j_captures.json`, the last tile of `captures_top.png`); large pictures of the prints do not wake it. On dark and mid fields shrinking helps every print: D1 in the capture light, centered on a dark field, goes from 0.037 at 200 model pixels (about 286 camera pixels) to 0.438 at 56 (80 camera pixels); on the bright field D3 stays below the lowest reportable score at every width (`e_f_scale.txt`). Over 16,800 placements of five pictures of the prints across size, position, field and exposure the best score is 0.5, and none reaches 0.85 (`e_n_sweep.txt`).
- **Compact things low in the picture, the more so when bright.** A plain white ellipse 24 × 12 model pixels, placed low right where the ceiling light sits, scores 0.562, higher than any real picture, and plain ellipses 48 model pixels wide and more stay below the lowest reportable score (`ellipse_placements.txt`). On a dark field a plain ellipse 40 × 20 responds most low in the picture, though below the lowest reportable score at all seven places tried (`e_c_synth.txt`); on the mid field it reaches the lowest reportable score, 0.0953, centered at (112, 150) (`ellipse_placements.txt`). Bright blobs on dark fields outscore dark blobs on bright ones by about 0.7 raw over 90 placements each (`e_k_polarity.txt`). On the 175 detector photos, none with a deer, the highest score is 0.438, on a small yellow object lying on a keyboard (`e_j_captures.txt`, `captures_top.png`).
- **The ceiling light, low in the upside-down picture.** Painted out of the 12 frames of the empty room, it leaves no box in any of them, and painted out of trial 1's ten frames it leaves no box in eight and a 0.095 box elsewhere in two (`e_e_lamp.txt`). Flipped top to bottom, frame 56 of the empty room scores nothing, and the twelve empty frames at most 0.119, on small boxes away from the light (`e_e_lamp.txt`, `e_d_preproc.json`, `preproc_boxes.txt`); the light's crop pasted elsewhere in the empty room scores 0.223 to 0.378 low in the picture, 0 to 0.321 in the middle and nothing near the top, and nothing turned upside down in place (`e_e_lamp.txt`). Its response follows its own patch: with the empty room's patch the light's cells give −0.75 to 0 raw whatever the rest of the picture, with D3's −3.25 to −4.25 on the print frames and −2.0 even in the empty room (`e_p_transplant.txt`).
- **An imitation of the capture light lifts the small D2 print on dark and mid fields, though not on a bright one.** Drawn onto the unlit D2 (three colored lobes, red, green and blue, white where they overlap, placed where the light falls in frame 18), with the print as the camera saw it, shrunk with the box filter, and the camera's JPEG settings, it raises the best score over the widths tried from 0.148 to 0.321 on a dark field, four steps of the ladder, and from 0.269 to 0.321 on a mid field, one step, and lowers it from 0.269 to 0.148 on a bright field, three steps. Through 20 variants of that path (the print either way round, the camera's JPEG settings or Pillow's quality 60, and five filters to shrink it), it raises the score on the dark field in every variant, by two to five steps, and on the mid field in every variant, by one to five, and lowers it on the bright field in all but one, by one to five steps; in that one, with the print mirrored, quality 60 and the bilinear filter, the score stays at 0.321 (`e_h_clean.txt`). Occlusion shows where each box's response comes from, but where a lit torso and the capture light's patch coincide it cannot tell them apart (`e_o_occl.txt`, `occl.png`).
- **Preparing the picture is as intended.** On eight frames the saved input differs from the saved JPEG, decoded with Pillow and prepared by a copy of the firmware's resize and scaling, by 0.6 to 0.7 of one input step on average in RGB order against 9 to 14 in BGR order, with gray bands of 57 above and below the picture, which is 114 scaled to the input (`e_d_preproc.txt`). Against the board's own input, Pillow's decode moves a frame's score by −2 to +3 steps of the ladder, a bilinear resize by −3 to +3, a box resize by −4 to +2 and stretching without the gray bands by −5 to +3, and each takes away or adds boxes on the lowest steps (`e_d_preproc.json`). A bilinear or box resize, or stretching, puts no box on a deer in any frame, and D1 at 2.5 in stays at 0 throughout; Pillow's decode keeps the boxes on D1's lit torso in trial 6 and takes away D3's head boxes (`preproc_boxes.txt`, `e_d_preproc.txt`).
- **The 8-bit arithmetic between layers.** With the stored, rounded weights and every layer's output kept in decimal arithmetic, the ceiling light scores 0.511 to 0.555 in the empty room, the coarsest layer stays silent and the middle one reaches at most 0.079; a frame's best response moves by −1.163 to +1.967 raw (`e_g_float.txt`, `e_g_float.json`). Noise the size of the weights' rounding moves it with a spread of 0.16 to 0.78 raw (`e_m_weights.txt`).
- **Counting only the boxes off the ceiling light, the two-of-three rule confirms at no level the firmware allows.** Nothing confirms at any level from 0.10 up; at 0.09, below the 10 to 100% the firmware allows, D3's head boxes confirm at frame 22 and D1's lit torso boxes in trial 6 at frame 60 (`replay_two_of_three.txt`).
- **A print farther from the lens.** `prediction_setup08.py` and `prediction_sweep.py` look ahead to the next test the team names, a retest with the same prints 1 to 2 ft from the lens and the ceiling light out of view. Set-up session 08's D1, about 9 in from the lens, the real frame nearest that distance, keeps a box on the animal of 0.1824 with the ceiling light painted out and 0.148 with only the sheet kept (`prediction_setup08.txt`). Over 1,512 placements of each picture at 40 to 78 model pixels the median score on the print is 0.095 for D1 in room light, 0.06 for D2 unlit, 0.269 for D1 in the capture light and 0.148 for the dog in the capture light (`prediction_sweep.txt`).

Scores move in fixed steps set by the model file. On the finest layer a reported score is sigmoid(k × 0.25) for a whole number k: 0.0953 (the lowest kept), 0.1192, 0.148, 0.1824, 0.2227, 0.2689, 0.3208, 0.3775, 0.4378, 0.5, and the first at or above 0.85 is 0.852; on the other two layers the steps are twice as large, the lowest kept 0.1192 and the first at or above 0.85 0.8808 (`e_q_misc.txt`, `recompute_oct29.txt`).

## Scope

The saved inputs are the board's exact model inputs; the edited copies, the crops of the saved JPEGs and the drawn shapes are made on a computer, and none of them was shown to the camera or run on the board. The reader agrees with the board at its outputs, on 74 scores and 69 boxes. Pillow's decode of the saved JPEGs, which many of the experiments use, moves a frame's score by up to three steps against the board's own decode, and a resize with Pillow's box or bilinear filter, which the experiments that shrink the prints add, by up to four, taking away or adding boxes up to 0.148 (`e_d_preproc.json`); the bicubic filter that `e_n_sweep.py` and `prediction_sweep.py` use for their larger widths is not measured. So a score through these paths may sit that far from the board's, and a difference that small, or a box up to 0.148, means nothing by itself. A difference that holds through every variant of the path tried does not come from what was varied. The imitation of the capture light is run through 20 variants of its path (`e_h_clean.txt`), which change the print's orientation, the JPEG setting and the filter that shrinks the print (five, bicubic among them); the decode is Pillow's in every one, so they do not test it. The imitation's lift on dark and mid fields holds through all 20, and its fall on a bright field through all but one. `e_g_float.py` keeps the stored, rounded weights, so it is not the model before its conversion to 8 bits, which is not run here. Frames within a trial are repeated looks at one scene, not independent trials.
