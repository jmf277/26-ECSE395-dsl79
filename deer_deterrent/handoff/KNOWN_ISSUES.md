# Known limits and next steps

- **No deer recognition:** model attempt failed the demonstration; growth heuristic accepts any suitable foreground region. Do not call this validated deer identification.
- **Fixed reference:** moving the camera, changing the scene or lighting can produce large changes. Recalibrate after moving it. Current compensation removes median brightness shift, not every exposure/shadow effect.
- **Approach assumption:** stationary, sideways, retreating, distant or rapidly entering animals may not meet the five-frame growth rule or ten-frame limit. Component overlap is a tracking heuristic and can merge/switch objects.
- **Thresholds are provisional:** 10%, 25% growth and 96-pixel gain were chosen from limited indoor evidence. A small nearby animal can look large. No field accuracy or long-duration stability statistics exist here.
- **PIR startup and hold:** warmup is 60 seconds; output must return LOW before a new HIGH. Continuous HIGH can mean hold/retrigger, nearby motion or wiring trouble. User found reversed PIR power/ground during debugging; correct supply polarity before testing.
- **Camera bus:** camera D3 must be wired to Feather D12/GPIO12. Initialization alone does not prove image data are correct; inspect decoded JPEGs. Saved images may be sideways; orientation is unchanged in current firmware.
- **RGB versus TFT:** GPIO35/36 are shared. Current firmware, the night-controlled detector and the recognition and laptop trial programs leave TFT off/CS high and use those pins for RGB. Do not enable TFT with RGB attached there. White is RGB mixing, not a calibrated photographic flash: at 5 in it makes a white patch about 3 to 4 in across with colored edges, washing out what lies under it, while a room 2 to 3 m away looks the same with it on and off; in the dark its spot is about 0.5, 1 and 1.5 m across at 1, 2 and 3 m and at most about 1 lux at its center at 1 m, too dim to light a deer for the camera.
- **Power:** complete battery operation was user-confirmed. On the MVP program the unit draws about 200 mA idle, 320 mA in a capture and 120 mA in the alarm, about 2 hours on the 450 mAh cell. About 150 mA of the idle draw is the buzzer's coil: the buzzer module is a low-level trigger, and the MVP program keeps GPIO10 low when silent, which holds the coil on; with the input held high when silent the cell would last about 8 to 9 hours. MCU stays awake; Wi-Fi off; camera uses standby. PIR supply matches the user's tested setup; its exact final VCC header was not recorded, so confirm it on the hardware rather than guessing.
- **Buzzer hardware:** signal remains GPIO10, but it was unplugged during later tests for lack of jumpers. Firmware alarm logs do not prove physical sound. Reconnect to test sound; deterrent effectiveness against deer is unmeasured.
- **Evidence gaps:** some early recordings began after events or ended mid-window; those claims are qualified in historical notes. Outdoors, on its battery, a person standing about 4 m away drew capture flashes and no alarm and a person walking up from about 3 m drew the alarm, one run each; outdoor accuracy against deer is not measured. The score-only model run did not retain its framing inputs; the recorded recognition trials keep every picture and exact model input.
- **Recognition trials:** the trial program needs USB to a running recorder, and "USB connected" means a host is attached, not that the recorder is running. With pictures and model inputs recorded, frames are about 2.1 s apart, about 0.9 s of it the USB transfer; `RECORD JPEG` leaves the inputs out. No recorded frame reached the 0.85 level, so no recognition decision started the response; `TEST OUTPUT` checks the path from a confirmed decision to the response by injecting the trigger that decision sends.
- **Laptop path:** it needs the trained float checkpoint, which is in neither repository, and has not been run with that model or on the board. Set the access-point test password before use.
- **Night-controlled detector:** written and tested on a computer; it has never been flashed to or run on the unit.
- **Shared response definition:** the recognition, laptop and night programs take the alarm waveform, pins and tone from `shared/alarm_pattern.hpp` and hold the buzzer input high when silent, which has not been run on the unit; the MVP program keeps its own copy of the same values, as it ran, with the input low when silent.
- **Next:** repeat centered small/large trials at measured distances; include cross-traffic, shadows, foliage/wind and empty-scene controls. Hold the MVP program's buzzer input high when silent, as the other three programs do, then document a battery run's duration and supply voltage with the input held high. Record failures, not only successful triggers.
